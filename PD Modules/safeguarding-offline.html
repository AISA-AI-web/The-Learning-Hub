Section 1

Why Safeguarding Matters

AISA is committed to a safe, inclusive and respectful environment for every student. Safeguarding is not a single role. It is a shared responsibility held by every member of staff.

⚖️ The guiding principle

The welfare of the child is paramount. It is a principle of UAE law and child-protection procedure that the protection and welfare of students must always come first.

📜 The frameworks we follow

Wadeema's Law: UAE Federal Law No. 3 of 2016 (Child Rights)
ADEK safeguarding regulations
ITFCP: Inter-agency framework for child protection
UK “Keeping Children Safe in Education” (KCSIE)

🧭 Our guiding principles

  • Equal protection for all students.
  • Students should feel safe physically, emotionally and digitally.
  • All concerns are taken seriously, acted on promptly, and kept confidential.
  • We work in partnership with families, authorities and agencies.

Safeguarding at AISA is reviewed annually by the Governing Board and the Safeguarding Committee.

Section 5 · Critical

Reporting & Whistleblowing

⏱️ The 24-hour rule

Report suspected abuse within 24 hours. All reporting goes to the DSL / Principal for your division.

🛡️ You are protected

  • Whistleblowers are legally protected in the UAE.
  • To stay anonymous, a reporter must explicitly request it.

⚖️ It is a legal duty

Under UAE law, a mandated reporter who fails to report is subject to fines and possible legal action. When in doubt, report.

Section 6

Responding to a Disclosure: the 5 R's

If a student discloses something to you, follow these five steps.

1

Receive

Listen carefully, without judgment.

2

Reassure

Affirm that speaking up was right. Don't make promises you can't keep.

3

React

Don't investigate. Ask only open-ended questions if you need to.

4

Record

Write the child's exact words, date, time and location, straight afterwards and not in front of the child.

5

Report

Notify the DSL and file the report internally with them.

✅ Do

  • Listen empathetically and non-judgmentally.
  • Observe the student; let them know they are believed.
  • Let them know who they can trust.
  • Work closely with the counsellor / admin.

🚫 Don't

  • Ask probing or leading questions.
  • Try to counsel the student yourself.
  • Tell people who don't need to know.
  • Assume it isn't true and fail to report.

🤐 On confidentiality with students

Never guarantee absolute confidentiality. It's better to say you might have to tell someone whose job is to keep them safe. Don't keep anything illegal, damaging or threatening covered up. Don't engage students who come to complain about other staff; pass it to the DSL.

Section 7

Types of Abuse & Risk Factors

It is everyone's responsibility to protect students' wellbeing, and at times you are the first responder. Harm can include threats from those responsible for a child's care: family members, guardians, or other adults in the student's life.

✋ Physical💔 Emotional🚫 Sexual / Exploitation🥪 Neglect

📈 Risk factors to be alert to

  • Social isolation
  • Trauma: death/illness, divorce or forced separation, witnessing violence
  • Neglect
  • Loss of protective community mechanisms

🔎 Harm isn't always visible

Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACE) research shows that children from affluent families can suffer hidden harm in less visible ways (Felitti et al., 1998). Stay alert across every community, and consider each situation through a thoughtful cultural lens.

Section 8 · Handle with care

Self-Harm & Suicide

It's okay to not be okay. Your calm, prompt response can save a life.

Self-harm is when someone hurts themselves to cope with very difficult feelings, painful memories or overwhelming situations. It can be a way to make invisible pain visible, to feel in control, or to express suicidal feelings without acting on them. Approach it without judgement.

👀 Possible signs of self-harm

  • Heavy scratching in one area; unexplained cuts or bruising
  • Cuts/scratches to arms, thighs or torso (easily hidden)
  • Bruising to knuckles or forehead
  • Oddly placed or repeated burn marks
  • Multiple plasters/coverings over one area

⚠️ Suicide warning signs

  • Talking, writing, texting or drawing about wanting to die
  • Planning (notes, researching methods)
  • Harming self; expressing hopelessness
  • Severe emotional pain or distress
  • Marked behaviour changes: withdrawal, sleep changes, anger, agitation

Responding to self-harm

  1. Remain calm.
  2. Listen with empathy. No judgement, and don't question their reasons.
  3. Inform the assigned counsellor.

🆘 If you believe a student may be suicidal

  1. Maintain direct physical supervision of the student.
  2. Immediately contact the Safeguarding Lead.

Do NOT wait until class is over or the end of the day.

Section 9

Digital Safety, Records & AI

📹 Security & digital safety

  • CCTV in all classrooms and main campus areas.
  • Visitor ID, sign-in and badge requirements.
  • No sharing of student data on personal devices or unapproved platforms.

🗄️ Confidentiality & record keeping

  • Secure digital storage, separate from academic files.
  • Access only by the DSL and one identified additional safeguarding member.
  • Staff must not keep personal copies of reports.

🤖 Essential guidelines for AI usage

  • Never input student names, contact details or unique identifiers.
  • Use only school-approved, privacy-verified AI platforms.
  • Follow data-minimisation principles.
  • Use pseudonyms (e.g. "Student A") instead of real names.
  • Remove identifying details before inputting student work.
  • Never share grades, health data or behavioural information.

🧑‍🏫 When to refer a student to a counsellor

  • You're concerned about a student's mood, behaviour or emotional needs.
  • A student shares, or you suspect, thoughts of self-harm.
  • A parent shares a challenging home/family circumstance.
  • A student lacks friendships or has poor social skills.
  • Known or suspected medical / mental-health needs.
  • You'd like support addressing a topic with your class.

Email the counsellor with your referral. Counsellors provide short-term support, crisis response and risk assessments. They do not counsel staff or issue consequences for students. Anything safeguarding-related still goes to the DSL.

Without an AISA email? Tell your facilitator or the student's class teacher in person, straight away, and they will make sure it reaches the DSL. If a child is in immediate danger, call 999.
