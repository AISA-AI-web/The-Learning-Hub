1How to read this Scope & Sequence

This document sets out what is taught, in what order, and which ADEK framework outcome each week secures. It is the planning instrument for the programme: a head of department should be able to plan a term from it, and an assessment lead should be able to audit coverage against the framework from it without opening a single lesson plan.

Every unit has the same shape

Across Grades 1 to 12 the Main Course follows one ten-week rhythm, and every Bridging Program follows one six-week rhythm. Learn the two shapes once and any grade’s table reads quickly.

ColumnWhat it tells you

Three tiers, three bars

  • Emerging The expected standard for Term 1 and the outcome each Main Course week secures.
  • Proficient The national entitlement standard and the target for the full year; the planned stretch in every lesson.
  • Advanced Further stretch, and the prior-grade bar that Bridging rebuilds.

The Bridging tables read differently

A Bridging table has the same first three columns, then two that differ. Bridging does not teach the current grade: it rebuilds each strand to the previous grade’s Advanced mastery, which is the readiness a student needs to begin the current grade at Emerging. So the fourth column gives the prior-grade Advanced descriptor, and the fifth gives the framework reference the published Teacher Pack prints for that week. A few weeks reach back two grades, where a strand does not exist at the immediately preceding grade; the reference column shows exactly which grade is cited.

On tiers and this term

Term 1 opens the academic year, so each Main Course unit is written to secure the Emerging descriptor as the expected standard, with Proficient and Advanced as planned stretch for every student. Proficient is the framework’s national entitlement standard and the target for the full year. Bridging works to a different bar: prior-grade Advanced. The Advanced descriptors for every grade and strand are set out in Section 5.

This release publishes taught materials for Grades 1 to 12. The framework’s KG1 and KG2 expectations are reproduced in Sections 2 to 5 without a week-by-week sequence, so that the Kindergarten entitlement is visible and Grade 1 teachers know exactly what their students should arrive holding.

3End-of-phase expectations

The macro level of the framework: what a student should be, and be able to do, at the end of each phase. Every grade sequence in Section 4 is a step toward the phase expectation set out here.

Phase 1 / KG, KG1–KG2

AI Aware Explorer

Students recognise how simple systems respond to actions in their everyday environment through physical interaction, unplugged observation and sensory play. Actual digital engagement with AI tools and technology is not expected in the early years.

Core competency milestone
Explores inputs and responses through play
What this looks like
Students distinguish between human senses and basic machine inputs and understand cause-and-effect relationships. Learning is unplugged with no direct AI tool use.
Strands at this phase
  • AI Conceptual Understanding
  • Data Awareness
  • Critical Evaluation & Informed Interaction
  • Ethics & Privacy
Core competencies
  • Early computational thinking
  • Pattern recognition
  • Structured communication
  • Responsible behaviour awareness
  • Emerging ethical awareness

Phase 2 / Cycle 1, Grades 1–5

AI Literate Thinker

Students understand foundational AI concepts, apply rule-based and multi-step logic, and interact with AI tools to generate and refine outputs. They begin to evaluate outputs critically and develop early awareness of how data influences results.

Core competency milestone
Applies rules, logic, and data to generate and evaluate outputs
What this looks like
Students progress from rule-based thinking to multi-step logic, develop understanding of how data influences outcomes, and begin interacting with AI tools to refine outputs.
Strands at this phase
  • AI Conceptual Understanding
  • Data Literacy
  • AI Solution Design & Development
  • Ethics & Responsible Use
Core competencies
  • AI conceptual literacy
  • Data awareness and organisation
  • Logical design and structured thinking
  • Introduction to AI interaction and prompting
  • Responsible AI use

Phase 3 / Cycle 2, Grades 6–8

AI Solution Builder

Students design, build, and evaluate AI-powered solutions using structured workflows and foundational machine learning concepts. They refine outputs through iteration and apply basic risk awareness.

Core competency milestone
Designs and improves AI-powered solutions using data, models, and workflows
What this looks like
Students build, evaluate, and refine solutions using structured processes and foundational ML concepts.
Strands at this phase
  • AI Conceptual Understanding
  • AI Solution Design & Development
  • Critical Evaluation & Informed Interaction
  • Ethics & Governance
Core competencies
  • AI conceptual understanding
  • Workflow design and prompting
  • AI solution creation and iteration
  • Output evaluation
  • Risk awareness

Phase 4 / Cycle 3, Grades 9–12

AI Systems & Solutions Designer

Students design, evaluate, and refine AI-powered solutions to address real-world problems. They integrate data, tools, and workflows using systems thinking, and ensure solutions are effective, responsible, and aligned with societal and governance expectations.

Core competency milestone
Designs and governs integrated AI systems
What this looks like
Students develop real-world solutions using systems thinking and ensure effectiveness, responsibility, and alignment with societal and governance expectations.
Strands at this phase
  • AI Systems Understanding
  • AI Solution Design & Development
  • Critical Evaluation & Informed Interaction
  • Governance, Ethics & Societal Impact
Core competencies
  • AI systems understanding
  • Systems integration
  • Solution design
  • Evaluation and validation
  • Governance and impact analysis
