Section 1

What "Workspace Studio" Actually Means

Google Workspace Studio is the umbrella term for Gemini's AI features built directly into the Workspace apps you already use — Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Drive, Meet, and Calendar — plus the standalone gemini.google.com workspace and reusable Gems (custom AI assistants).

It's not a new app. It's an AI side-panel and prompt layer that sits inside the tools where your work already lives, plus a small set of new surfaces (Gems, Audio Overviews, Deep Research) that use the same Gemini engine.

Workflow 1 · Gmail

Drowning in parent emails? Triage, summarise and reply in 90 seconds.

Gmail's side panel can summarise long threads, draft replies in your tone, and extract action items from a parent or admin email. Three taps replace twenty minutes of re-reading.

Example interface

Try this prompt in "Help me write"

Reply professionally and warmly. Acknowledge the parent's concern, offer two meeting times this Thursday or Friday between 3–4pm, attach the differentiated reading list, and confirm I'll loop in the Year 5 Lead. Keep it under 120 words.

Time saved per teacher per week

~3.5 hrs

Based on triaging ~40 parent / admin emails per week. Most of the saving is in not re-reading 14-message threads.

⚠️ Read before sending. Gemini drafts can omit nuance, get tone wrong for a sensitive parent, or invent attachments that don't exist. Every email goes out with your name on it — read every line.

Workflow 5 · Gems

Build a Gem once, run it 100 times

The single highest-leverage feature in Workspace Studio.

A Gem is a custom, reusable Gemini assistant you configure once with detailed instructions, persona, examples, and source files. Then it lives in your Gem gallery and you call it any time without re-typing context.

If you find yourself writing the same kind of prompt more than three times — build it as a Gem.

🛠️ Anatomy of a good Gem

  1. ① Persona & tone: "You are a calm, experienced Year 5 reading coach who…"
  2. ② Specific task: "When given a text, produce 3 questions: one literal, one inferential, one evaluative."
  3. ③ Format constraints: "Output as a numbered list. Tag each question with its DOK level."
  4. ④ Worked example: One sample input + the exact output you want. This is the single biggest quality lever.
  5. ⑤ Knowledge file (optional): Attach your scope & sequence, rubric, or style guide so it answers in context.

A team of teachers building 6–8 shared Gems for the year covers about 70% of the recurring AI tasks across a department.

Workflow 7 · Meet

"Take notes for me" — and stop dreading meeting minutes

Inside any Google Meet, click the sparkle and choose "Take notes for me". At the end of the meeting, every attendee gets a Doc with a structured summary, key decisions, and action items tagged by owner.

Section 9

Three rules that keep you (and your students) safe

🔒

1. Stay inside the AISA domain

Use Gemini signed in with your @aisa.edu account, in the Workspace side panels. Don't paste student data into personal Gemini, ChatGPT, or any free public model.

👁️

2. Read every output

Hallucinations happen — wrong dates, fabricated quotes, miscalculated formulas. Anything that goes out under your name carries your professional accountability.

🧭

3. Keep the pedagogy yours

Use Gemini for first drafts and tedium. Cognitive sequencing, differentiation, and judgements about specific students remain teacher work — see the AI Ethics module.
