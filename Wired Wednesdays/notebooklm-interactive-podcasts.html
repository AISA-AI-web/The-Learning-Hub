Frame the why · 3 min

From "AI explains it" to "AI converses with it"

A NotebookLM Audio Overview turns your sources — a unit plan, a chapter, a video, a research paper — into a two-host podcast that explains, banters about, and questions the material. The new Interactive beta lets a listener join the conversation: pause, ask a question, get an answer in voice, then resume.

🎙️

Two AI hosts

Conversational, sometimes funny, grounded only in the sources you give them.

🎚️

Steerable with prompts

Focus on a chapter, set a reading level, demand UAE examples, change tone.

🗣️

Interactive (beta)

A listener taps "Join", asks a question by voice, and the hosts answer in real time.

Presenter note · the one-liner

"Think of it as a study group on tap — one your weakest reader can join without feeling exposed, and your fastest reader can push deeper than your lesson plan ever would."

Quick poll (30 sec)

"In the chat — have you tried a NotebookLM Audio Overview before? Yes / No / Heard of it." Read the room before diving in.

Step 2 · 6 min

Find & add sources

NotebookLM is only as good as what you feed it. Quality, diversity, and currency of sources determine whether the podcast sounds like a study group or a guess. We'll add sources four ways in the live demo:

🌐

A · Discover with web search

Click Discover in the Sources panel. Describe the topic in plain English; NotebookLM returns a curated set of public web pages you can review and add with one click.

Discover prompt Find authoritative, student-friendly sources on the cell cycle and mitosis for Year 9 biology. Prefer sources with diagrams, real cell images, and analogies. Avoid pages aimed at university students.
▶

B · YouTube links

Paste a YouTube URL as a source. NotebookLM transcribes the audio and treats it like any other text source — the hosts can cite it, quote it, and weave it into the podcast.

Try these in the demo:

  • • Amoeba Sisters — Mitosis
  • • Crash Course Biology — Mitosis: Splitting Up is Complicated
  • • A teacher-made screencast (your own!)
🔗

C · Paste a URL

Any public web page — a textbook chapter on a school site, a news article, a Wikipedia entry, an exam board resource. NotebookLM ingests the readable text.

Watch-out

If the page is paywalled or requires login, NotebookLM won't see what you see. Test by previewing the page in an incognito tab first.

📎

D · Upload from device

PDF, .docx, .txt, Google Doc/Slide/Sheet, .md, audio files. Drag-and-drop or click Upload. Most useful for your own unit plan, textbook chapter scans, lecture slides.

Presenter rule of three

Aim for 3+ sources of 2+ formats before generating. One PDF chapter + one YouTube + one curriculum page is the minimum that gives the hosts something to argue about. One source = a monologue.

Source hygiene

  • No PII. Never upload sources containing student names, grades, IEP details, or parent emails — even into your private notebook.
  • Copyright. Treat scans of a textbook the same way you would in any other tool. Personal teaching use — yes. Redistribution — no.
  • Bias check. If every source comes from the same publisher, the podcast will sound like one publisher. Mix it up.
04

Step 3 · 4 min

Open the Studio & customize the podcast

In the Studio panel on the right, find the Audio Overview card. Click Customize before Generate — this is where the magic is. A blank generate gives you a competent, generic podcast. A customized prompt gives you a podcast that fits your class.

What you can steer with a prompt

  • 🎯 Focus area: "Spend most of the time on prophase & metaphase."
  • 🧠 Reading level: "Aim for a Year 9 reading level — concrete language, short analogies."
  • 🎭 Persona/tone: "One host plays the curious student, the other the patient teacher."
  • 🌍 Context: "Use UAE examples where possible; respect Islamic values."
  • 🚫 What to avoid: "Do not cover meiosis — we cover that next week."
  • ⏱️ Length cue: "Keep it under 8 minutes."
  • ❓ Embedded questions: "End with three questions a student should be able to answer."
Demo prompt · the workhorse You are producing a 6-minute Audio Overview for Year 9 biology students in Abu Dhabi. Focus: the four phases of mitosis (prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase). Use concrete analogies a 14-year-old would understand. Reference UAE contexts where natural. One host plays a curious learner who asks the questions a Year 9 would actually ask; the other host is the patient teacher who answers without jargon. End with three retrieval questions students should be able to answer after listening. Do not cover meiosis — that's a separate lesson.

Tip: paste this exact prompt during the live demo so the audience can see the impact of customization side-by-side with a blank generate.

Three more prompt patterns worth showing

Pre-reading

"Pre-teach the 8 key vocabulary terms before students read the chapter. Don't reveal answers to the chapter's review questions."

Revision

"Treat this as exam revision. Quiz the audience throughout. Pause for 3 seconds after each question so the listener can answer in their head."

Debate

"The two hosts disagree. One defends Theory A from Source 1; the other defends Theory B from Source 3. Steelman both sides."

Discuss · 60 sec

"For your next unit — would a pre-reading, revision, or debate framing fit best?" Park answers in the chat — we'll come back to choice in the impact section.

Live demo plan — three Qs to try (audience can suggest theirs)

  1. Clarifying: "Can you say that last bit again, simpler?" — tests rephrasing.
  2. Analogy: "Give me a sports analogy for what just happened." — tests creativity within bounds.
  3. Off-topic: "What's the capital of France?" — tests grounding. The hosts should politely refuse or redirect to the sources.

What it isn't (yet)

  • It's beta: occasionally glitches, occasionally re-explains. Frame it that way for students.
  • It's grounded in your sources — if the source doesn't cover it, the hosts shouldn't either. Verify.
  • It is not a 1:1 tutor in the sense of tracking a student over time. Each interactive session is fresh.

Discussion · 4 min

Why it matters for students

Pull this section forward if generation is taking a while. The tool is the easy part — the "so what for students?" is the conversation worth having.

🎚️

Student choice & voice

Some students learn by reading, some by talking, some by listening on the bus. A podcast version of the unit isn't replacing the chapter — it's a parallel lane. Students choose how they engage; the goal stays the same.

"Read the chapter or listen to the 6-min podcast or watch the YouTube playlist — come to class ready to discuss question 3."

🌱

Self-study growth

The interactive beta makes self-study active. A student stuck on anaphase at 9pm at home can ask — not search, not scroll — ask, and get an answer grounded in your unit. The reluctant question-asker in class can rehearse questions in private first.

♿

Accessibility & multilingual learners

Audio is huge for EAL students, dyslexic readers, and learners with visual processing issues. Combined with the option to prompt the hosts to use simpler vocabulary, NotebookLM punches above its weight as a differentiation tool.

🧭

Metacognition & question quality

When students ask the hosts a question and get an answer, they get instant feedback on whether their question was clear. Over time, that quietly trains asking good questions — the highest-value academic skill we don't usually teach.

⏱️

Time & equity

A 6-minute podcast version of a 30-page chapter is a gift to the student who works a part-time job, supports siblings at home, or just thinks better when their hands are free. The richer student already has tutors; this is closer to giving everyone one.

🧑‍🏫

Teacher development

Generating a podcast forces you to name your sources. That alone is a quiet curriculum-audit habit — what am I actually teaching from? Is my source set diverse enough? It's PD-by-stealth.

Open question to the room

"If you generated one of these for next week's unit and shared it on Friday afternoon — what's the worst way it could play out? What's the best? What guardrails would you want in place?" Capture answers; these become next week's session pre-prep.

Three classroom plays you could trial next week

Pre-lesson

Share the podcast 48h before class. Open the lesson with the three retrieval questions the hosts ended with.

Revision week

Generate one per topic. Students pick three to listen to, then run the interactive Q&A as guided self-study.

Absent student

A student misses Tuesday's lesson. Share the notebook; they catch up by listening + interacting on the way home.

08

Q&A · 4 min

FAQ

These are the questions that came up across the early pilot — have them open as the conversation moves. Take live questions first; fall back to these if there's silence.

Is it free? Do students need accounts?
You need a Google account to create notebooks — your AISA Google account works. Students don't need an account to listen to a podcast you've shared as a link or download. To use the interactive beta themselves, they need to be signed in — with their AISA account, that's already provisioned.
Is what students say to the hosts logged or visible to me?
No. The interactive conversation is between the listener and the model — you don't get a transcript of what each student asked. Treat it like a study partner, not a formative assessment tool. If you want evidence of engagement, ask students to write down the question they asked and the answer they got.
How accurate are the hosts? Can they hallucinate?
They're grounded in the sources you upload — they cite, they paraphrase, they shouldn't invent facts that aren't in the sources. They can simplify in ways that lose nuance, especially with technical sources. Spot-check the first podcast on each unit against your authoritative source. Don't ship it to students without listening end-to-end at least once.
What languages are supported?
Audio Overviews are available in 50+ languages, with English the most mature. Arabic is supported. You can prompt the hosts to speak in a specific language — useful for parallel EAL versions of the same unit.
Can I edit the podcast after it generates?
Not directly — there's no waveform editor. But you can regenerate with a refined prompt, which is almost always faster than editing audio. If you need surgical edits, download the .mp3 and use any audio editor (Audacity, Descript, etc.).
How long does generation take? And can I cap the length?
Usually 2–5 minutes for a 6–8 min podcast. Length depends on source volume and your prompt. Yes, prompt for length — "Keep it under 6 minutes" generally works. Audio Overviews max out around 10–12 minutes in current builds.
What about student data privacy?
NotebookLM uses your sources to generate output for you; Google's stated policy is that your personal notebook content is not used to train models. That said: never upload anything containing student PII (names, grades, IEP notes, behavioural records) into any AI tool, including this one. Stick to curriculum and public materials.
How does this compare to ChatGPT's voice mode or Gemini Live?
Voice modes in ChatGPT/Gemini are general-purpose conversations — the model can talk about anything. NotebookLM Interactive is source-grounded — the hosts will only answer from what you uploaded. For classroom use that's usually a feature, not a limitation: it keeps the conversation tethered to your curriculum.
Can students upload their own sources and create their own notebooks?
Yes — with their AISA Google account, students have access. That opens a separate conversation about student-led use: revision packs they build for themselves, podcast-style reflections on a topic they choose. Worth a dedicated Wired Wednesday later in the term.
What if I don't have a "unit pack" ready — what's the minimum I need?
Honestly — three sources of mixed format. A textbook chapter PDF, a YouTube video, and one authoritative web link gets you a podcast worth listening to. You can always come back and add more.
