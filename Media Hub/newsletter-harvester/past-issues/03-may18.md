Note from the Team

#### A note from the Head of AI & Innovation

Quick follow-up on last week's note. The class visits I mentioned kick off this Monday, and I want to be as upfront as possible about how they'll run — same energy as last week: no surprises, no judgement, no theatre. The most useful thing I can do is share the exact form I'll be filling in, the days I'll be visiting which grades, and the reason I'm doing this at all. So this issue is short and almost entirely about that.

To repeat what I said last week: these visits are not evaluative. The form below has no place for a teacher name, no rating of your teaching, and no narrative section — it's a handful of structured checkboxes about what AI looks like in the room, not about you. The data is aggregate. It feeds the AI & Innovation integration plan for next year. That's the whole story.

And to say it one more time, because it matters: please don't prepare anything for me. Don't change the lesson, don't queue up an AI demo, don't shuffle activities. I want to see what a normal Monday/Tuesday/Wednesday looks like in your classroom. That's the data that's actually useful.

The Form Itself

#### Classroom AI Integration Snapshot — shared for full transparency

This is the exact form I'll be filling in during each visit. I'm sharing it openly because the easiest way to make a classroom visit comfortable is to remove the mystery: this is what's checked, this is all that's checked, your name is not on it.

- ✓ No teacher name. The form doesn't ask for it and I'm not adding it.

- ✓ Non-evaluative. There is no "rate the teacher" question — only structured prompts about the AI activity in the room.

- ✓ What it captures: grade level, subject area, type of AI integration observed (if any), student engagement level, and the AI tool used (if known).

- ✓ "No observable AI integration" is a perfectly valid answer — and one I genuinely expect to record often. We're at the start of a multi-phase plan; the goal is a true picture, not a rosy one.

- ✓ This is phase one. Future phases will look at different things — this form is calibrated only for where we are right now.
